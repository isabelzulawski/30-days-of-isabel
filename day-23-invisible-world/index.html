Inside your phone

Inside your phone is a chip, a tiny piece of silicon packed with tiny switches. Scroll to zoom in.

Fitting millions or even billions of parts onto a chip is called very large scale integration, or VLSI.

Who makes the chips?

Chip designers draw the blueprints. Manufacturers like Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) build the chips on round slices of silicon called wafers.

TSMC calls one way of making chips “28 nm.” That’s the name of a technology generation. How small is 28 nm?

How small are we talking?

A nanometer (nm) is one billionth of a meter.

1× magnification

Sizes are approximate. The 60 nm and 28 nm markers show example lengths. Real switches vary in size.

About 2,850 lengths of 28 nm could fit across one hair.

A switch inside a chip

A transistor is a tiny part that controls electricity. In a chip, it can act like a switch. Try flipping one to see how two states can represent a 0 or a 1.

How a chip remembers
